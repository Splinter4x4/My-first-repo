> - **Sole trader / partnership:** taxed at your personal marginal rates (up to 47% incl. Medicare) → potentially **$90,000+**.

### Legitimate levers to pull before 30 June (subject to accountant sign-off):

1. **Top up superannuation** — only $4,000 paid so far. The FY26 concessional cap is **$30,000 per person**. Paying up to the cap (and it must *clear the super fund* before 30 June) is deductible — potentially **$26,000 deduction** = thousands in tax saved. 2. **Write off genuine bad debts** — that 152-day INV-0414 and any other uncollectable invoices, formally written off **before 30 June**, reduce taxable income. Document the decision.
3. **Prepay expenses** — rent, subscriptions, advertising, insurance for up to 12 months ahead can often be deducted now under the small-business prepayment rules.
4. **Bring forward purchases / instant asset write-off** — any tools/equipment you genuinely need: buy and *install ready for use* before 30 June to claim under the current instant asset write-off threshold (confirm the FY26 threshold with your accountant).
5. **Pay June-quarter super for staff early** — normally due 28 July; paying before 30 June makes it deductible this year.
6. **Division 7A check (if a company)** — if you've drawn money out of the company personally, make sure it's structured as wages, dividends, or a compliant loan to avoid a deemed-dividend tax hit.
7. **Defer income where commercially sensible** — given the huge jump, your accountant may suggest timing of late-June invoicing, but only where it makes business sense.
