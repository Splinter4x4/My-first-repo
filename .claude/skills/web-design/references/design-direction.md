# Choosing a direction

A direction is a single organizing idea that every later decision answers to.
Without one, a page drifts toward defaults. Pick one of these archetypes (or a
deliberate blend of two), name it, and let it govern type, color, layout, and
motion. The goal is coherence: every choice should feel like it came from the
same hand.

## Editorial

Reads like a well-made magazine or essay. Strong typographic hierarchy, a real
text face set at a comfortable measure, generous margins, drop caps or pull
quotes, restrained color. Best for writing-heavy sites, journalism, thoughtful
product stories.

- **Type:** serif text face + a characterful display face; large, confident headlines.
- **Color:** paper-like neutrals, ink, one quiet accent.
- **Layout:** asymmetric columns, wide margins, content that breathes.

## Swiss / grid

Precise, rational, grid-driven. International Typographic Style: tight alignment,
a visible or strongly-felt grid, Helvetica-lineage or a clean grotesque,
flush-left ragged-right text, lots of structure.

- **Type:** a single excellent grotesque, used at a few decisive sizes.
- **Color:** black/white plus one primary (often red).
- **Layout:** rigorous modular grid, baseline alignment, no decorative filler.

## Brutalist / raw

Deliberately unpolished and structural. Visible borders, system or monospace
type, high contrast, unapologetic density. Reads as confident and indie when
done with intent (not as broken).

- **Type:** monospace or a stark grotesque; system fonts are on-theme.
- **Color:** stark — black on white, or one loud color.
- **Layout:** boxed sections, hairline or heavy borders, exposed structure.

## Technical / precise

For developer tools, infra, data. Conveys rigor: monospace accents, fine rules,
dense but legible tables, muted palette with a single signal color, subtle grid.

- **Type:** clean sans for prose, monospace for data and labels.
- **Color:** cool neutrals + one signal accent (lime, cyan, amber).
- **Layout:** tight grid, generous data density, hairline dividers.

## Warm / organic

Human, friendly, tactile. Rounded forms, warm neutrals, a humanist typeface,
hand-drawn or photographic texture, soft but intentional motion.

- **Type:** humanist sans or a soft serif.
- **Color:** warm off-whites, earthy or candy accents, never pure black.
- **Layout:** relaxed rhythm, gentle asymmetry, real photography.

## Maximalist

More is more, on purpose. Layered type, clashing-but-curated color, overlap,
texture, movement. Hard to do well — needs a strong eye — but the opposite of
generic when it lands.

- **Type:** mixed faces, dramatic scale contrast, type as image.
- **Color:** bold, multi-color, high energy, held together by one rule.
- **Layout:** overlap, collage, broken grid with intent.

## Retro-specific

Anchored to a clear era (90s web, Y2K chrome, 70s print, 8-bit). Powerful
because the references are concrete and recognizable. Commit fully — half-retro
reads as a mistake.

- **Type:** period-accurate faces.
- **Color:** period palette.
- **Layout:** period conventions, reinterpreted for modern screens.

## Picking well

- Match the direction to the **content and audience**, not your preference.
- When unsure, default to **editorial** or **swiss** — both are hard to make
  look generated and forgiving to execute.
- Write the direction down in one sentence ("precise technical, monospace
  accents, single lime signal color on cool grey") and check every later
  decision against it.
