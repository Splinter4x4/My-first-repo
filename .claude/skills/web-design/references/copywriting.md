# Copywriting

Copy is the loudest tell of all. A page can have great type and color and still
scream "generated" because the words say nothing. Generic copy is worse than a
generic layout because visitors *read* it.

## The banned vocabulary

These words and phrases mark copy as machine-written. Avoid them:

- empower, unlock, supercharge, elevate, seamless, seamlessly
- revolutionize, transform your workflow, take it to the next level
- "Welcome to our platform", "Your one-stop solution"
- cutting-edge, best-in-class, world-class, robust, leverage (as a verb)
- "In today's fast-paced world…"

They're empty: they could describe any product. Replace them with specifics.

## Write specific, not impressive

- Say what the thing actually **does**, in plain words.
- Use concrete details — numbers, names, real outcomes, real constraints.
- One true, specific sentence beats three sweeping benefit statements.
- Headlines can be plain and confident. They don't need to "sell."

Generic: *"Empower your team with our seamless, best-in-class platform."*
Specific: *"Ship changes from a PR to production in under 90 seconds."*

## Voice

- Match the direction and audience. Technical audiences want precision; consumer
  audiences want warmth and clarity. Neither wants hype.
- Pick a consistent person and tense and hold it.
- It's fine to have a point of view. Bland is what reads as generated.

## Microcopy

- Buttons: name the action ("Start the trial", "See pricing"), not "Get
  started" / "Learn more" by reflex.
- Empty states, errors, and confirmations are a chance for voice — write them.
- Labels over placeholder-only fields; placeholders disappear and aren't
  accessible.

## Never ship filler

- No "Lorem ipsum." If you don't have real content, draft realistic content and
  flag it as a placeholder for the user to replace — don't leave latin in.
- Don't pad to fill a section. If there isn't enough to say, the section
  shouldn't be that big.

## Quick gut check

Read the page aloud. If it sounds like a press release written by someone who's
never used the product, rewrite it with specifics.
